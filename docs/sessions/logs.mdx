A **Logs** tab opens already following. A log is opened to see what is happening now, and a console
you have to switch on first is a step in front of the answer.

## Pod logs

A pod's **Logs** tab is in the **OBSERVE** group. It starts live: it reads the recent tail of the
container and then follows it. Turning **Live** off stops the follow and shows a one-shot read of
the same container, window and tail instead, which is what holds the view still while you read.
Turning it back on starts a new follow.

/>

The tab stays mounted when you move to another tab of the same pod, so a follow survives that — and
it ends when you leave the page.

## The toolbar

The toolbar is one row, and it decides _which_ lines are shown.

- **Container** — which container of the pod to read. See [Choosing a container](#choosing-a-container).
- **Since** — how far back to read: **5 minutes** (the default), **15 minutes**, **1 hour** or
  **All logs**. It bounds the follow, the Live-off read and the download alike. On a quiet pod the
  default window can hold far fewer lines than the tail asks for; choose **All logs** to see further
  back.
- **Search**, with its three toggles — see [Searching](#searching).
- **View** — how the lines read. See [The View menu](#the-view-menu).
- **Live** — follow, or hold a snapshot.
- **Download** — the download icon at the end. See [Downloading](#downloading).

Changing the container, the Since window or the tail starts the read again and clears the console,
since what is on screen belongs to the old choice.

## Choosing a container

The **Container** picker lists every container in the pod by name, in the order the pod runs them:
init containers first, then the app's own containers, then any ephemeral ones. It opens on the first
entry.

Only the pod's regular containers can be read. Choosing an init or ephemeral container gives the
error `container "<name>" of pod "<namespace>/<pod>" not found`. The **Shell** tab has the same
picker and the same limit.

:::caution[Known issue]
On a pod with an init container, the tab opens on the init container and fails. Pick an app
container from the list. ([#333](https://github.com/kubermeister/kubermeister/issues/333))
:::

There is no option to read the log of a container's previous run.

## Following and scrolling

While Live is on, the console keeps the newest line in view. Scroll up and it stops following, so
new lines do not pull the view away from what you are reading; a **Jump to latest** button appears
at the bottom, and it, or scrolling back to the end, resumes the follow.

The status bar under the console says what you are looking at: **streaming** or **snapshot**, the
number of lines, **(filtered)** when the search is hiding some, and **following** or **not
following**.

A follow that fails shows its error in red above the lines. When the container exits, the follow
simply ends, and nothing restarts it: turn **Live** off and on again, or reopen the tab.

## Workload logs

A Deployment, StatefulSet, DaemonSet, Job or CronJob has a **Logs** tab that follows **every pod it
owns at once**.

<Figure
  name="workload-logs"
  alt="A deployment log console merging every pod it owns, each pod’s lines in its own colour."
  caption="Each line starts with its pod's name, in that pod's colour."
/>

There is no API call for "the logs of this deployment", so the app opens one follow per pod, merges
the lines in the order they arrive and gives each pod a colour and its full name at the start of the
line. It differs from a pod's tab in a few ways:

- **Each pod's first app container is followed.** There is no choice of container: the picker's place
  shows the number of pods, and the pods it lists are there for reference, not as a filter.
- **Live off shows nothing.** There is no snapshot for a workload; the console is empty until you turn
  Live back on.
- **The set of pods is checked on the refresh interval** — **Live data refresh** in
  [**Settings › General**](/docs/reference/settings/), 12 seconds by default — rather than watched.
  When it changes, every follow restarts and the console is cleared, each pod reading its tail again.
  A pod replaced in a rollout is therefore picked up within one interval, not at once.
- When a pod's follow fails, the error names the pod: `<pod>: <message>`.

## Searching

Search is in the toolbar. It matches the **message** only — not the timestamp, and not the pod name
on a workload's lines. It ignores case unless you turn on **Aa**, and reads the text literally unless
you turn on **.\***, which makes it a regular expression.

- **By default the search narrows the console.** A line the search misses is gone, not merely
  unhighlighted, and the status bar adds **(filtered)**.
- **Highlight** is the other mode: every line stays and the matches are marked where they sit, which
  is often the only way a hit is legible — the stack trace under the line that named the error. Its
  toggle is the highlighter icon beside **.\*** and **Aa**, not in the View menu, because it changes
  the search the way they do.
- **An unfinished regular expression filters nothing** rather than emptying the console
  mid-keystroke. The toolbar says **Not a valid pattern yet** in red until it parses.

## The View menu

How the lines are _read_ sits behind the **View** button:

- **Wrap long lines** — wrap instead of scrolling sideways. Off by default.
- **Show timestamps** — a pod stamps every line by default; a workload does not, since each of its
  lines already spends a column on the pod's name.
- **Tail** — how many lines each follow reads before it starts following: **100**, **500**, **1,000**
  or **10,000**. A pod reads 500 by default. A workload reads 100 **per pod**, and the Tail you
  choose applies per pod there too.

Until you touch them, timestamps and tail are left to the screen. Once you change one, your choice
applies on every log console from then on. These are preferences about your window rather than the
cluster, so they are stored locally rather than in the settings file.

## The log buffer

A live follow keeps a bounded buffer and drops the oldest lines once it is full. The size is **Log
buffer** › **Buffered lines** in [**Settings › General**](/docs/reference/settings/): **2k lines**
(the default), **10k lines** or **50k lines**. On a workload's tab the limit is on all its pods'
lines together, not per pod.

The setting is read live: changing it trims differently from the next batch of lines rather than
restarting the follow and losing what is on screen.

Only the rows in view are rendered, so a buffer of tens of thousands of lines costs a screenful of
the page.

## Downloading

What **Download** saves depends on the tab.

- **A pod** — the app asks the API server for the container's log within the selected **Since**
  window, not only the tail on screen, and saves it as `<pod>-<container>.log` with a timestamp on
  every line. With the default window that is the last 5 minutes; choose **All logs** first for the
  whole log. A log over 8 MiB keeps its newest part, cut on a line boundary so the file never opens on
  half a line, and a **Log downloaded** notice says the oldest lines were left behind.
- **A workload** — the lines in the console as they stand, after the search, saved as
  `<workload>-pods.log`, one line each as pod, timestamp and message. There is no read from the API
  server, so it holds what the buffer holds; with Live off it is empty.

## Ending a follow

Leaving the page ends its follows. So does switching context or namespace, which closes the page
back to its list, and quitting the app. Nothing reconnects a follow by itself.
