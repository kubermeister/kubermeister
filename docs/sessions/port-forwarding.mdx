Start a forward from a pod or a Service. Every forward is then listed in the **top bar** and stopped
from there, rather than from the page that happened to start it — because that page is often not the
one you are on when you want it gone.

## Starting a forward

- **From a pod** — its **Network** tab, in the **INSPECT** group, under **Port forward**.
- **From a Service** — its **Ports** tab, under the table of ports.

**Target** is a list of the ports the object declares: a pod's container ports, or a Service's
ports. A port that is not declared cannot be chosen, and a pod that declares none says **This pod
declares no container ports.** instead of offering a forward.

**Local** is the port on your machine. Leave it empty to use the same number as the target. Then
**Start**. The line under the control says `Listening on 127.0.0.1:<local> → <target>`, and
**Start** becomes **Stop**; the two ports cannot be changed while the forward runs.

The listener is bound to **127.0.0.1** only, so nothing else on your network can reach it.

- A local port outside 1–65535 gives `error: enter a valid local port (1–65535)`.
- A local port already in use gives an error such as `error: listen EADDRINUSE …`. The forward stays
  in the top bar with that error until you stop it.
- Starting the same forward on the same local port again hands back the one already running rather
  than binding twice. A different local port is a second forward.

If a forward cannot start at all — the pod has gone, or the Service has no ready endpoint — it
disappears again with no message left on screen.

## Forwarding to a Service

A forward to a Service resolves to a **ready endpoint per connection**, and the top bar shows which
pod the connections currently go to.

That is the difference that matters. A forward aimed at one pod stops working when that pod is gone:
it stays listed, and each new connection fails with `<pod> has no ready pod to forward to`, shown in
red on its row, until you stop it. (A pod recreated under the same name, as a StatefulSet does, is
reached again, though the row keeps showing the last error.) A forward aimed at a Service survives the rollout, because each new connection picks a
pod that is ready right then.

:::caution[Known issue]
A Service forward sends traffic to the Service's `port` number on the pod, not to its `targetPort`.
For a Service mapping port 80 to target port 8080, the forward reaches the pod's port 80. Until it is
fixed, forward to the pod itself when the two numbers differ.
([#334](https://github.com/kubermeister/kubermeister/issues/334))
:::

## The top bar

/>

The **Port forwards** button, with a cable icon, counts the forwards that are listening. It appears
once there is a forward to list or one to offer. Its popover lists each forward with its kind, name
and `http://127.0.0.1:<local> → <target>`, and two buttons: one opens that address in your default
browser, the other stops the forward. A forward whose connections are failing has a red dot and the
error under its name.

Forwards are held outside the page that started them, so navigating away does not end one, and
neither does switching namespace.

## Remembered, but never reopened unasked

Every forward you start is remembered against the context it was started on — the most recent 20,
across all contexts. When you are on that context and no forward to the same object is open, the top
bar offers it again, greyed, with a play button; pressing it starts the forward and says **Forwarding
&lt;name&gt; again**.

They are never reopened automatically. Reopening a local port on your machine without asking would be
the app deciding something about your machine, which is not its call. There is no way to remove a
remembered forward from the list; it falls off once 20 newer ones have been started.

## What ends a forward

A forward runs until one of these:

- **You stop it** from the top bar, or with **Stop** on the page that started it.
- **You switch context.** Every forward is stopped and leaves the top bar, since each names a pod of
  the cluster being left.
- **You choose another kubeconfig, or change the proxy or the CA bundle.** Every stream shares the one
  live connection, so every forward ends and leaves the top bar.
- **The window reloads**, or the app quits.

Losing the network does not end a forward either: the listener stays up, and the connections made
while the cluster is unreachable fail, with the error on the forward's row.

## When the app quits

Quitting ends every port forward, shell session, log follow and node drain, and none of them are
restored on the next launch; forwards are only offered again, as above. So quitting asks first:

> **Quit Kubermeister?**
>
> Port forwards, shell sessions, log follows and any drain in progress end with the app, and none of
> them are restored on the next launch.

The dialog's text is the same whether or not anything is running. **Cancel** is the default button,
so Return or Escape leaves everything running; **Don't ask again** takes effect only when you choose
**Quit**. It asks on **Quit Kubermeister** (⌘Q) on macOS, on **File › Quit** on Windows and Linux,
and there on closing the window too, since closing it quits the app. On macOS closing the window does not quit the app, and does not ask, but it
still ends the forwards and other sessions that window held.

Turn the dialog off or back on with **Confirm before quitting** in
[**Settings › General**](/docs/reference/settings/).
