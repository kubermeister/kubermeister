Kubermeister reads the kubeconfig you already have. If `kubectl get pods` works in your terminal,
the app works too.

## 1. Check the connection

The top bar tells you which context you are on, and the dot beside it whether the cluster answers
and its nodes are ready. If something is wrong with the kubeconfig or the context, a notice appears
there with the ways out: **Try again**, **Choose kubeconfig…**, and **Use default kubeconfig** when
you have chosen a kubeconfig of your own. The app itself always loads, even when no cluster will
answer.

## 2. Find a workload

The app opens on the cluster summary. Open **Workloads › Deployments**. The list is live — it
watches the API server rather than polling, so a rollout appears as it happens, and the footer says
"live" while the watch is connected. Type in the search box to narrow to the rows you want.

## 3. Read its logs

Open a deployment and go to its **Logs** tab. It is already following, and it is following _every_
pod the deployment owns at once, merged in arrival order and coloured by pod. There is no API call
for "the logs of this deployment", so the app opens one stream per pod. The set of pods is re-read
at the refresh interval, so a replaced pod stops being followed and its replacement starts.

Search narrows the console. Turn on **Highlight matches**, the highlighter button beside the search
box, if you would rather keep every line and mark the matches where they sit.

## 4. Shell into a pod

Open one of its pods from the **Pods** tab and choose the **Shell** tab. The session opens with the
tab and ends when you leave, which is how a shell is closed — nothing is left attached in the
background.

:::caution[Known issue]
On a pod with an init container, Shell and Logs open on the init container and fail. Pick an app
container from the list. ([#333](https://github.com/kubermeister/kubermeister/issues/333))
:::

## 5. Forward a port

On a pod, the **Network** tab starts a forward to one of its declared container ports; on a
Service, the **Ports** tab does the same for one of its ports. Forwards are listed under the port
forwards button in the top bar and stopped from there, wherever you started them. A forward aimed
at a Service picks a ready pod behind it for each new connection, so the next rollout does not end
it.

:::caution[Known issue]
A Service forward sends traffic to the Service's `port` on the pod, not its `targetPort`, so it only
works where the two are the same.
([#334](https://github.com/kubermeister/kubermeister/issues/334))
:::

## 6. Look at the cluster

**Overview › Cluster summary** puts node health, usage, recent events and alerts on one screen. The
usage charts are sampled while the app is open — they start empty on every launch and reset when
you switch context, so they never claim history the app does not have.
